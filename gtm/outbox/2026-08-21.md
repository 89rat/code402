## LinkedIn (~190 words)

Yesterday we posted about the 122 payments our system "lost" under stress — money that
settled on-chain while our records timed out. Today, the engineering that answers them.

The most expensive sentence in payments infrastructure is "it probably settled." Hope is
not a reconciliation strategy. So we built one that refuses to guess:

Every hour, a sweep takes every unresolved payment claim and asks the blockchain
directly — the only party that can't be wrong about whether money moved. It reads the
authorization's on-chain state, then the AuthorizationUsed / AuthorizationCanceled event
logs, and resolves each claim exactly three ways: settled (with the transaction hash
attached to the record), canceled (payer revoked — alarm raised), or expired unused.

And for the case the events can't yet answer? It stays unresolved. The system is
explicitly designed to say "unknown" rather than guess — because in payments, a wrong
answer is worse than a late one.

All four resolution scenarios were proven with real money and real transaction hashes —
including a payer canceling on-chain mid-flight, and the hourly sweep itself re-driving
a stuck settlement to completion.

The evidence is public: https://code402.dev/proof — including the failure that motivated
all of it.

Live reconciliation telemetry, updated hourly: https://code402.dev/v1/ops/stats
