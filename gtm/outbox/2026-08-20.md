## LinkedIn (company page — first post)

Today we're launching Code402 into public view — the way we built it: with the failures included.

Code402 is a settlement layer for machine-to-machine payments. AI agents pay APIs per call over HTTP 402 (the x402 standard) in USDC on Base. Funds move directly from payer to merchant — we never hold them. Non-custodial by construction.

That constraint is the whole product. When you can't refund mistakes, every failure mode must be answered with engineering:

• Exactly-once settlement per payment authorization — race losers receive the byte-identical stored response
• A reconciler that treats the blockchain as the source of truth: every unresolved claim resolves against on-chain state as settled, canceled, or expired — all four scenarios proven with real money and transaction hashes
• Cryptographic entitlements: if the chain proves you paid and we never served you, your next identical request runs free, bound to the original input
• XDR-1 receipts: every paid call returns a signed receipt any third party can verify offline — no call to us required

The part we lead with: during stress testing with 1,000 real settlements, the system "lost" 122 payments — transfers settled on-chain while our records timed out. We exported all such cases into a public regression corpus and built the reconciler that resolves them. Every defect became a test.

Live, now: https://code402.dev — and the evidence, not the marketing: https://code402.dev/proof

Operational telemetry is public too (reconciler runs, backlog, breaker state): https://code402.dev/v1/ops/stats — at this moment: backlog 0, breaker closed, 212 machine-payable services indexed in the companion Atlas.

Code and full review trail: github.com/code402dev
