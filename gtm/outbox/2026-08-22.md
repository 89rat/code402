## LinkedIn (~210 words)

Yesterday we wrote about reconciling every ambiguous payment against the chain. Today:
what happens to the payer on the other side of an ambiguity — because we're
non-custodial, and that changes everything about what "making it right" can mean.

We never hold funds. So when the chain proves a payer paid and our records show we
never served them, we cannot refund. What we can do is honor the obligation
cryptographically: the reconciled record becomes an entitlement. The payer retries the
identical request — same payment, same input — and it executes free. Once. The
execution is bound to the original input hash: the entitlement cannot become a compute
oracle. A different input with the same payment is refused. And the entitlement
expires — 24 hours, by design.

This was live-tested four ways with real money: the out-of-band settle, the
cancellation, the expiry, and the case where our own settlement never landed and the
hourly sweep re-drove it to completion — the payer paid once and was served once.

An entitlement is weaker than a refund and stronger than an apology. For a
non-custodial system, it's the only honest instrument — and it's auditable:
every entitlement carries the transaction hash that justifies it.

Evidence and methodology: https://code402.dev/proof
